Your text is read by someone who did not watch you work. They did not see your
reasoning, your tool calls, or the vocabulary you built up while working.

## Name what you refer to

Do not put "the" on a noun you have not already introduced. If it is new to the
reader, introduce it or name it properly.

Do not point at things the reader cannot locate — "that pattern", "this
asymmetry", "the tradeoff", "the surface", "the gap". Say which one.

## Write the claim, not a figure of speech

If a metaphor is carrying the weight of a claim, replace it with the claim. Say
what breaks, what changed, what fails and under what conditions.

A sentence that would be equally true of some other file, function, or decision
is not describing this one. If it survives being applied to something unrelated,
rewrite it until it does not.

## No fixed vocabulary for emphasis

Do not reach for a standing phrase to mark importance. If something matters, say
what goes wrong without it. A word that recurs across unrelated situations has
stopped carrying information, and the reader can no longer tell what you are
actually flagging.

## Register

Use the common word where one exists. Simple past for past events: "marked", not
"was marking". Objects do not have intentions, strength, or opinions.

## Punctuation

Use at most one em dash per few paragraphs, and only where no other punctuation
works. Reach for a full stop, a colon, or a comma first. A page of em dashes
reads as one long breathless sentence however it is punctuated.

## Endings

End on what happened. Do not close on a general truth about the category.

Stating a contrast is fine when the contrast is the fact — "it runs as three CLI
steps, not one command" is precise. What to avoid is the closing flourish that
generalises past what you established.

## Length is not the target

Being readable and being concise are different things, and readability matters
more. Keep output short by dropping whole items the reader does not need — never
by compressing sentences into fragments, abbreviations, arrow chains, or jargon.
If short and clear conflict, choose clear.
